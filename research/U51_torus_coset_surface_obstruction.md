# U51: every torus-coset surface in the degree-70 Fermat fourfold is paired, and none can hit the target block

**Status:** restricted structural obstruction proved below, with independent exact enumeration of all 15 pairings. This is not a proof of nonalgebraicity and does not solve the Hodge conjecture.

Date: 2026-09-16. Base: current Hodge repository after U49/U50. Target character

    alpha=(1,20,24,42,61,62) mod 70.

## 1. Setup

Let

    X = {x0^70+x1^70+x2^70+x3^70+x4^70+x5^70=0} in P^5.

Consider an irreducible surface Z whose dense open part is a translate of a two-dimensional algebraic subtorus of the dense projective torus (C*)^6/C*. Such a Z is a torus-coset surface. This includes the natural binomial/toric complete-intersection candidates that arise from three homogeneous Laurent relations.

We ask whether the primitive class of Z can have nonzero projection to the alpha eigenspace.

## 2. Restricting the Fermat equation to a torus coset

Choose torus parameters t=(t1,t2). On the dense coset, after choosing a projective normalization, each coordinate restricts to

    xi = ci * t^{vi},

where ci is nonzero and vi is a character of the two-dimensional torus. Hence

    Q|_Z = sum_i ci^70 * t^{70 vi}.

Distinct Laurent monomials on a torus are linearly independent. Since Q vanishes identically on Z, the six indices split into blocks according to equality of the exponent vectors vi, and within every block B one has

    sum_{i in B} ci^70 = 0.

Thus every block has size at least 2. Therefore there are at most three distinct exponent vectors.

On the other hand, because Z is genuinely two-dimensional in projective space, the coordinate ratios xi/xj generate a rank-two character lattice. Hence the affine span of the distinct vi has rank 2, so at least three distinct exponent vectors are required.

Consequently there are exactly three blocks, each of size 2. Therefore every torus-coset surface contained in X is of the paired type: after reindexing, the six coordinates split into three pairs whose torus characters are equal. The constants in each pair satisfy one degree-70 cancellation relation.

This conclusion uses only Laurent-monomial independence and dimension; it does not assume a priori that the surface is one of the classical linear P^2 cycles.

## 3. Necessary character condition for a nonzero alpha projection

Fix a pairing P={{i1,j1},{i2,j2},{i3,j3}}. The connected diagonal stabilizer of the corresponding torus coset contains the annihilator of the within-pair difference lattice generated by

    e_i1-e_j1, e_i2-e_j2, e_i3-e_j3.

For a cycle class invariant under this stabilizer to have nonzero alpha-eigenprojection, alpha must be trivial on the stabilizer modulo the projective diagonal character. Equivalently, there must exist c in Z/70 such that for each pair {i,j},

    alpha_i + alpha_j == 2c mod 70.

This is the exact projective version of the stabilizer condition; it is weaker than requiring each pair sum to be 0.

## 4. Exhaustive check of all pairings

There are exactly 15 perfect matchings of six indices. Exact Wolfram enumeration checked every pairing and all 70 values of c.

Result:

    perfect matchings checked = 15
    compatible matchings      = 0

Therefore no paired torus-coset surface has a nonzero alpha projection.

As a consistency check, if one allows arbitrary set partitions with no singleton blocks, there are 41 possibilities: 15 of type 2+2+2, 15 of type 2+4, 10 of type 3+3, and one block of size 6. Only the 3+3 partition

    {1,42,62} | {20,24,61}

(up to the fixed coordinate labels) and the one-block partition satisfy the weaker projective character congruence. But those patterns have at most two distinct torus characters, so their projective images have dimension at most 1 and cannot be surfaces. This recovers the earlier 35/35 arithmetic observation while explaining why it cannot define the needed torus surface.

## 5. Consequence

No irreducible torus-coset surface in the dense torus of X_70 can represent the target Hodge block.

This strictly contains several earlier negative ansatz families:

- classical three-pair linear cycles;
- binomial complete intersections whose dense part is a torus translate;
- saturated rank-three Laurent-relation attempts whose restriction of Q vanishes identically.

It does **not** rule out:

- non-toric complete-intersection surfaces;
- non-complete-intersection surfaces such as scroll-type pullbacks;
- genuinely coupled higher-rank matrix factorizations;
- the Horikawa mixed-product correspondence route;
- arbitrary algebraic surfaces on X.

## 6. Research implication

The Favero–Kelly complete-intersection formula remains useful, but any successful complete-intersection cycle for alpha must be non-toric: its defining equations cannot all be Laurent binomials defining a torus coset. The next matrix-factorization search should therefore target genuinely coupled polynomial entries rather than monomial/binomial toric data.
