# U35: correct-dimension saturated rank-2 toric surface lattice carrying alpha

Status: exact lattice construction of a surface ansatz. Geometric nonemptiness/closure and nonzero alpha-period remain unproved. This does not solve the Hodge conjecture.

Date: 2026-09-14.

## Correction-driven search

After correcting U34, a torus surface in the Fermat fourfold should generically be obtained by imposing two independent homogeneous Laurent relations in the ambient projective torus, then intersecting with X_70.

Search primitive homogeneous relation vectors with entries in [-3,3] for a saturated rank-2 lattice L=<r,s> satisfying

    alpha in span_mod70(r,s,1).

Exact enumeration produced the witness

    r=(2,2,-3,-1,-1,1),
    s=(3,0,-3,1,-2,1).

Both have coordinate sum zero. They have rank 2 and saturation index 1 (gcd of all 2x2 minors equals 1).

Most importantly,

    alpha == 45 r + 17 s  (mod 70),

with no projective diagonal shift required.

Thus the diagonal stabilizer of the torus defined by r and s is contained in ker(alpha).

## Explicit Laurent equations

For nonzero constants lambda,mu, the relations can be written

    z0^2 z1^2 z5 = lambda z2^3 z3 z4,

    z0^3 z3 z5 = mu z2^3 z4^2.

These equations involve all six coordinates and are not pair relations.

## Dimension

The projective dense torus in P5 has dimension 5. Two independent Laurent relations cut it to dimension 3. Intersecting with the Fermat hypersurface generically gives dimension 2, exactly the desired surface dimension.

The relation matrix together with the projective diagonal has rank 3. Its rational nullspace has dimension 3; one computed basis is

    (-8,-4,-3,0,0,15),
    (1,-7,-9,0,15,0),
    (-14,8,-9,15,0,0).

This provides a route to an explicit three-parameter torus parametrization before imposing the Fermat equation.

## Why this survives previous obstructions

- correct generic dimension for a codimension-2 cycle on X_70;
- saturated relation lattice;
- alpha is in the mod-70 row span, so the stabilizer character obstruction vanishes;
- both equations meet the dense torus and involve multiple coordinates;
- not generated by complementary pairs or the paired-binomial construction eliminated earlier.

2. Audit boundary components and irreducibility.
3. Compute the alpha-component of its cohomology class. Stabilizer compatibility permits a nonzero component but does not guarantee one.
